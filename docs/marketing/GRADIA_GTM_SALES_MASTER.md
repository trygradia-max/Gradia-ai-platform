## Executive brief

### The category

**Gradia is the agentic operating system for auto detailing businesses.** It connects
lead intake, customer and vehicle context, conversations, quotes, scheduling, human
approvals, pipeline work and reviewed operational memory around one primary Gradia
Agent.

Gradia is not positioned as:

- a generic AI chatbot;
- only a digital receptionist;
- a replacement for every human decision;
- a collection of disconnected “agents” with separate memories;
- a full repair-order, parts, labor or shop-management suite today;
- an autonomous outbound-calling or bulk-marketing platform today.

The useful contrast is not “AI versus no AI.” It is **a connected, governed operating
loop versus a pile of inboxes, notes, calendars and follow-up tasks that depend on the
owner remembering everything.**

### The one-line promise

> Gradia helps detailing shops turn more inquiries into organized, reviewable booked
> work—without giving an AI uncontrolled access to customers or the business.

### The short pitch

Detailing shops lose time and opportunity between the first inquiry and the booked
job. Leads arrive through different channels, customer and vehicle details live in
different places, follow-up depends on memory, and the owner becomes the routing layer
for the whole company. Gradia creates one operational system for that work. Its Agent
can capture and organize the lead, assemble the relevant customer, vehicle, service
and schedule context, prepare the next action, and route sensitive work to a person.
The shop controls what the Agent may read, suggest, queue for approval or eventually
execute within explicit rules.

### The commercial wedge

Lead with the expensive operational moment that every qualified prospect recognizes:

> “What happens to a new inquiry when you are polishing a car, driving, talking to a
> customer, or finished for the day?”

The wedge is **lead-to-booking operations**, not “AI transformation.” The expansion is
the connected operating layer: inbox, CRM, quoting, calendar, team controls, reviewed
memory, measurement and additional bounded playbooks.

## 1. Market narrative

### The old operating model

A local service business can have good demand and still run an unreliable revenue
process. The customer sees one shop, but behind the scenes the work is fragmented:

1. an inquiry arrives by form, text, Meta, email or phone;
2. somebody has to identify the customer and vehicle;
3. somebody asks the missing questions;
4. service and condition details must become a responsible quote or next step;
5. availability must be checked against the real calendar and capacity;
6. exceptions need a human decision;
7. the booking, conversation, pipeline and follow-up must agree;
8. the owner must notice anything that failed or went unanswered.

Most tools solve one screen or channel. The owner remains the integration layer.
That creates fragile growth: more inquiries can produce more interruptions, more
duplicate entry and more dropped context instead of more confirmed work.

### The new operating model

Gradia treats the inquiry-to-booking journey as a durable operating loop. The Agent
does not receive unlimited authority from a prompt. It operates through bounded tools,
tenant-scoped context, consent and policy checks, explicit approval modes, stable
action identities and an auditable execution boundary.

The strategic story is:

> **From owner-dependent follow-up to a reviewable operating system.**

This story is stronger than “automate your shop” because it names the actual constraint
and preserves trust. The shop can begin with AI Off or Suggest/Approval Required,
prove the workflow, and grant narrow autonomy only where the owner intentionally
chooses it and safety rules permit it.

### Why now

- Customers expect quick, coherent replies across multiple channels.
- Detailers need operational leverage without adding administrative overhead before
  revenue supports another hire.
- Generic AI can draft text, but it does not know which shop, customer, vehicle,
  service, schedule, consent rule or approval applies.
- Businesses increasingly want AI assistance while retaining human authority over
  pricing, customer communication and schedule commitments.
- The differentiating work is moving from “generate an answer” to “safely complete
  and prove an operational outcome.”

Do not support “why now” with invented market-size, response-time or conversion
statistics. Add third-party figures only after recording the source, methodology,
publication date and exact allowed claim.

## 2. Problems Gradia solves

Use these as problem-led messaging pillars. Validate which two or three matter most
to each prospect instead of reading the full list.

### Revenue and customer-experience problems

- New inquiries wait while the owner is working, driving or off-hours.
- Leads fall between channels or depend on someone remembering to follow up.
- The same customer repeats vehicle, service and timing details to multiple people.
- Incomplete qualification creates slow back-and-forth or irresponsible quoting.
- Follow-up is inconsistent after the first reply or quote.
- A busy inbox makes it hard to distinguish a real buying signal from general noise.
- A prospect may receive a response, but the business still lacks a clear next action.
- Booking, confirmation and pipeline status can disagree.

### Operational problems

- Customer, vehicle, quote, conversation and calendar context are scattered.
- Owners copy information between messages, notes, spreadsheets and calendars.
- The owner is the default escalation point for every exception.
- Staff cannot see what needs review, what is waiting and what actually completed.
- Calendar availability can look open without representing staff or resource capacity.
- Failed or uncertain provider outcomes disappear or appear completed too early.
- Duplicate webhook or form deliveries can create duplicate work without replay
  protection.
- Teams lack a consistent, inspectable operating process as they grow.

### AI trust problems

- Generic chatbots can invent price, availability, identity or completion.
- “Autonomous” software often hides which action it took and why.
- A prompt may blur the boundary between customer content and system instructions.
- Past approval does not mean future permission, but simplistic systems treat it that
  way.
- Businesses need AI that can be paused, constrained and audited without making the
  rest of the CRM unusable.
- Learning from corrections is dangerous if one edited message silently becomes a
  business-wide rule.

### Measurement problems

- Shops often do not know their starting response, follow-up or administrative load.
- Software dashboards count drafts and attempts as if they were business outcomes.
- ROI claims compare unlike time periods or replace unknowns with zero.
- Owners cannot tell whether a lead was qualified, handed off, booked, blocked or
  lost to an uncertain provider outcome.

### The buyer-facing summary

> Gradia reduces the operational gaps between inquiry, context, decision and booking.
> It gives the shop one place to see what happened, what needs a person, and what the
> Agent is actually allowed to do.

## 3. Ideal customer profile

### Primary ICP

An owner-led auto detailing business that:

- operates from one active location or one mobile service area;
- is solo or has a small team of owners, managers and technicians/detailers;
- receives enough inbound inquiries that response and follow-up are recurring work;
- books services that require customer, vehicle, condition, location or timing context;
- uses a mix of phone, text, website, Meta, email, calendar, CRM, spreadsheet or notes;
- experiences missed calls, delayed replies, duplicate entry, inconsistent follow-up
  or owner bottlenecks;
- wants measurable operational improvement, not a novelty chatbot;
- accepts a controlled, approval-first rollout and provides structured feedback;
- can name a primary workflow owner and participate in onboarding;
- is willing to use verified channels and comply with consent requirements.

### Highest-priority early adopter signals

Prioritize shops where at least four are true:

- the owner personally answers or routes most new inquiries;
- inquiries arrive through three or more sources;
- the shop misses or returns calls after the fact;
- follow-up lives in memory, personal reminders or a spreadsheet;
- the team re-enters customer/vehicle details;
- quoting requires gathering several pieces of information;
- the owner cannot quickly report inquiry-to-booking performance;
- the business is adding staff, capacity or lead volume;
- the prospect is already paying for fragmented CRM, texting, scheduling or answering
  tools;
- a recent consulting conversation exposed response, intake, handoff or efficiency
  pain;
- the owner wants AI but insists on approval and visibility.

### Secondary ICP

- Newer shops establishing a professional operating process before lead volume grows.
- Established solo mobile detailers who need fewer interruptions and better continuity.
- Staffed shops that need role clarity, approvals and a common customer history.
- Shops evaluating a receptionist but preferring a human-first Call Copilot path once
  that capability passes its production gate.

### Anti-ICP / disqualifiers

Do not force-fit accounts that:

- require multiple active locations, cross-location routing or enterprise workforce
  optimization immediately;
- require a full repair-order, parts, labor, inspection or accounting system today;
- require autonomous outbound calling or bulk campaigns at launch;
- refuse consent, identity, channel-registration or human-review requirements;
- demand custom one-off development before proving the core workflow;
- have almost no inbound lead flow and no operational problem to measure;
- will not identify an owner/champion or participate in onboarding;
- need a specific unsupported CRM integration as a day-one condition;
- expect guaranteed revenue or conversion results;
- want the Agent to make unbounded discounts, pricing exceptions or booking decisions;
- serve primarily as a read-only user with no repeated operational workflow.

### ICP hypothesis to test

The strongest initial segment is likely an owner-led shop with **meaningful inbound
volume, one location/mobile area, a small team, fragmented communication and a visible
owner bottleneck**. Treat that as a hypothesis until the first 10-account cohort shows
which firmographic and behavioral traits predict activation, repeated use and retained
value.

## 4. Buyers, users and their jobs

### Owner / economic buyer

**Wants:** fewer dropped opportunities, less administrative interruption, control over
customer-facing work, visibility into operations and a system that scales beyond the
owner's memory.

**Fears:** an AI embarrassing the shop, unauthorized messages or bookings, surprise
usage charges, another tool nobody uses, migration pain and losing control of customer
data.

**Core job:** “Help me run a more reliable inquiry-to-booking operation without hiring
or supervising a new administrative layer for every increment of growth.”

### Manager / operational champion

**Wants:** one needs-review queue, clear assignments, consistent context and fast
resolution of exceptions.

**Fears:** low-quality drafts, duplicate work, unclear authority and an automation
creating cleanup work.

**Core job:** “Show me exactly what needs attention, the evidence behind it, and the
safe next action.”

### Staff member / frontline user

**Wants:** current customer and vehicle information, assigned work and fewer repeated
questions.

**Fears:** surveillance, opaque decisions or being blamed for an AI action.

**Core job:** “Give me the context and permitted actions for my work without making me
learn the owner's entire process.”

### Customer / affected participant

**Wants:** a timely, accurate response; no need to repeat information; clear next steps;
and respect for communication preferences.

**Trust requirement:** Gradia must not treat an inbound message as blanket marketing
consent, invent price/availability, or continue contact after a valid opt-out.

## 5. Jobs-to-be-done

### Functional jobs

- When an inquiry arrives, capture it once and keep its source and identity evidence.
- When information is missing, identify the responsible clarification instead of
  guessing.
- When a response or CRM change is prepared, show exactly what will change and why.
- When a booking is proposed, check the relevant schedule and capacity again at
  execution.
- When a human must decide, place the item in a visible, accountable review queue.
- When an operation succeeds, fails or becomes uncertain, preserve the truthful state.
- When the team corrects an Agent proposal, retain the feedback without silently
  publishing a new rule.
- When performance is reviewed, compare labeled starting evidence with like-for-like
  observed evidence.

### Emotional jobs

- Let the owner step away from the phone without feeling that every inquiry is at risk.
- Give the team confidence that automation will not outrun permission.
- Replace “I think we followed up” with an inspectable operational record.
- Make AI feel like a controlled employee system, not an unpredictable black box.

### Social jobs

- Help the shop present a professional, responsive customer experience.
- Help the owner lead with a repeatable operating process rather than heroic memory.
- Demonstrate modern capability without sacrificing local-business trust.

## 7. Positioning and differentiation

### Positioning statement

For owner-led detailing businesses that lose time and opportunity between inquiry and
booking, Gradia is an agentic operating system that connects customer and vehicle
context, communications, quotes, scheduling, approvals and operational memory. Unlike
a generic chatbot or isolated receptionist, Gradia works through bounded business
operations with explicit human control, evidence and truthful outcome state.

### The four differentiators

#### 1. Operational completion, not chat alone

The Agent is designed around the lead-to-booking loop and domain records—not a chat
window that leaves the owner to copy the answer into the business.

#### 2. Governed agency

Every tool is subject to tenant, role, connector, consent, policy and risk controls.
Sensitive work starts approval-required. A business can use the CRM even when AI is
Off.

#### 3. Detailing-specific context

The operating model connects customer, vehicle, service, quote, capacity, schedule
and follow-up context. It is not a general-purpose prompt wrapped in a dashboard.

#### 4. Reviewed memory and evidence

Gradia separates authoritative records, conversation evidence, candidates and
approved reusable memory. Corrections do not silently become policy. Actions and
claimed outcomes retain their evidence chain.

### Competitive framing without unsupported claims

Use category contrasts rather than unverifiable superiority claims:

| Alternative | What it does well | Gap Gradia addresses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shared inbox | Centralizes messages | Does not by itself connect qualification, quote, booking, approval and reviewed memory |
| Traditional CRM | Stores records and pipeline | Often still depends on humans to interpret every inquiry and coordinate the next step |
| Calendar/scheduler | Manages time slots | Does not own customer identity, communication consent, qualification or exception review |
| Digital receptionist | Answers or routes calls | May remain a channel-specific layer rather than the shop's connected operating system |
| Generic AI assistant | Drafts and reasons across text | Lacks Gradia's tenant, role, vehicle, service, policy, execution and audit contracts |
| Human admin/receptionist | Provides judgment and flexible service | Gradia can organize repetitive work and evidence while keeping people on exceptions; it is not positioned as eliminating the team |

Do not publish named competitor comparisons until product marketing verifies the
competitor's current public capabilities, pricing and terms.

### Category language hierarchy

1. **Primary:** agentic operating system for detailing businesses.
2. **Plain-language translation:** one connected system that helps turn inquiries
   into organized, reviewable booked work.
3. **Mechanism:** one Gradia Agent, bounded tools, unified communications, approvals,
   CRM/calendar context and reviewed memory.
4. **Proof:** exact workflow demo, audit/evidence, baseline and verified pilot outcomes.

Avoid “AI employee that runs your whole business” until the full runtime and production
acceptance justify it. “Digital employee” may describe the configured Agent only when
paired with its bounded job, tools, limits and human controls.

### FAQ answers

**Is Gradia a digital receptionist?**

Inbound voice is one Gradia playbook, not the whole product. Gradia is the operating
system connecting inquiry context, CRM, quotes, scheduling, approvals and follow-up.
Voice remains account-gated until its production acceptance requirements pass.

**Will it message or book customers without approval?**

Customer-facing actions begin approval-required. Owners may later enable specific,
bounded operations where the product and account are eligible. Consent, suppression
and hard schedule conflicts cannot be overridden by an approval or prompt.

**Does Gradia replace my CRM?**

Gradia includes the customer, vehicle, pipeline, quote, calendar and activity spine

## 15. Objection handling

### “I already have a CRM.”

Your CRM may remain useful. The question is whether it owns the operational gap from
new inquiry through context, qualification, approval, quote, schedule and follow-up.
Gradia is positioned around that connected loop, not just record storage. During
discovery, map what the current CRM handles and do not promise an unsupported
integration.

### “I can just use ChatGPT.”

A general model can draft text. It does not inherently know the authenticated shop,
the correct customer and vehicle, current consent, approved services, capacity,
effective policy or whether an external action actually completed. Gradia wraps model
work in those operating and authority boundaries.

### “I do not want AI talking to my customers.”

Start with AI Off, Read/Suggest or Approval Required. The business remains usable as
CRM and operations software without autonomous customer communication. Narrow
autonomy is an explicit later choice, not the default.

### “Will this replace my receptionist?”

That is not the promise. Gradia organizes and advances repeatable operational work and
keeps people on judgment and exceptions. Voice is one optional capability. A human-
first Call Copilot is planned but is not yet sold as available.

### “What if it books the wrong time or price?”

Quotes and bookings begin approval-required, and availability must be rechecked at
execution. Unknown or stale required-calendar state blocks automatic confirmation.
Hard capacity conflicts cannot be bypassed by an ordinary approval. Do not imply the
full production loop is accepted until it is.

### “Why usage pricing?”

The workspace fee covers the operating system and included capacity. Usage aligns
additional cost with verified work and provider consumption. Digital outcomes do not
stack inside one workflow, drafts/failures/unknowns are not billable outcomes, and the
owner controls a monthly cap.

### “Will my bill run away?”

The approved design warns at 50%, 80% and 100% of the cap and stops new automatic paid
work at 100% while retaining approvals, evidence, exports and revocation controls. The
default cap is $100 and may be raised by the owner to $500. State clearly that live
billing awaits final acceptance.

### “Is my customer data used to train a global AI?”

Gradia's approved architecture keeps operational memory tenant-isolated and separates
records, evidence and reviewed reusable memory. Do not make a broader provider-training
or contractual claim until the final privacy policy, processor terms and production
configuration are verified.

### “Can you guarantee more bookings?”

No. Gradia can improve the operating process and measure selected outcomes, but demand,
offer, price, service quality and customer choice remain outside the software. We set a
baseline and report verified like-for-like changes instead of guaranteeing revenue.

### “Why should I join before it is broadly launched?”

Founder Pilot accounts receive a controlled implementation around their workflow,
direct feedback access, a 14-day build/test period and 12-month price protection for
structured participation. They also accept that only listed, readiness-tested channels
are activated and that the product is still completing production acceptance.

## 16. Founder Pilot charter

### Pilot objective

Prove that Gradia can improve one defined inquiry-to-next-action workflow for a real

### Cohort

- first 5–10 controlled shops;

1. **Fit and baseline:** workflow map, Starting Point evidence, success measures.

   use fixtures/dry runs.
3. **Readiness:** provider/channel, policy, consent, billing and support gates.
4. **Controlled activation:** one account and channel at a time with kill switches.
5. **Observe:** monitor outcomes, exceptions, usage and human edits.
6. **Day-30 review:** like-for-like comparison, qualitative feedback and continuation
   decision.

### Minimum success evidence

- users complete the relevant workflow without founder database intervention;
- active channels and limitations are recorded;
- one or more useful outcomes have exact evidence and are not drafts/attempts;
- no cross-shop or unauthorized action succeeds;
- uncertain provider outcomes remain visible and are not blindly retried;
- usage and charges reconcile to the applicable ledger before live collection;
- champion can explain where Gradia saved work or improved visibility;
- baseline comparison does not invent missing data.

### Failure / stop conditions

- consent or channel-binding cannot be verified;
- an unauthorized/cross-tenant action is possible;
- billing or usage cannot be reconciled;
- provider outcomes are presented as complete without evidence;
- no customer-side champion participates;
- the account depends on excluded scope;
- operational burden exceeds demonstrated value without a credible correction path.

## Closing narrative

Gradia should win by being more operationally serious than a chatbot and more
intelligent than a passive database. Its marketing must make that distinction feel
simple:

> A detailing shop should not need the owner to manually connect every inquiry,
> customer, vehicle, quote, schedule decision and follow-up. Gradia gives the shop one
> approval-controlled Agent and one evidence-backed operating loop for that work.

The brand promise is not “AI does everything.” It is **work moves forward, people stay
in control, and the system tells the truth about what happened.**
