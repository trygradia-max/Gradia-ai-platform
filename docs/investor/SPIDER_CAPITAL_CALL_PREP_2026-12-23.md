## Three-minute company pitch

### Problem

Automotive service businesses operate across calls, texts, web forms, calendars, CRMs and employee knowledge. Revenue leaks when leads are missed, estimates are not followed up, appointments are not recovered and customer records are incomplete. Traditional software stores information but still requires employees to coordinate the work.

### Product

Gradia observes approved triggers, retrieves business context, proposes or executes a bounded next action and records the outcome. Customer-facing and sensitive actions remain controlled through permissions, approval modes, tenant isolation, audit evidence and reviewed memory.

### Initial wedge

The initial customer is a 5–25-location detailing, tint/PPF, collision or general-service group. The first deployment focuses on one measurable workflow: missed-lead recovery, quote follow-up and appointment conversion. We prove recovered revenue and faster response before expanding.

### Why Gradia can win

- Gradia begins with workflows and outcomes rather than a generic chatbot.
- Consulting creates customer access and implementation knowledge.
- The platform is designed around governed execution rather than unrestricted model actions.
- The same operational spine expands across workflows and locations.
- Usage and outcome evidence support value-aligned pricing and measurable ROI.

### Current stage

The platform has substantial foundations in place, including normalized lead intake, versioned agent definitions, durable workflow foundations, approval-controlled actions, reviewed/versioned memory, onboarding measurement, tenant isolation and commercial billing. The team is completing signed-in usability acceptance, provider activation, production billing acceptance and remaining launch controls before controlled customer deployment.

Be explicit: Gradia is not broadly launched and does not yet have meaningful software revenue. Consulting relationships and deals in audit/discovery are distribution and learning advantages, not contracted SaaS revenue.

### Vision

The long-term opportunity is to become the execution layer across distributed automotive service networks. A business should be able to define how work is performed, what Gradia may do autonomously, what requires approval and how every action connects to an operational and financial result.

## Five-minute demonstration

Use one synthetic shop and one believable customer journey. Do not improvise with live providers or real customer data.

1. **Starting Point:** Show how Gradia records the operator's baseline without inventing ROI.
2. **Lead arrives:** Show a normalized lead or conversation entering the system.
3. **Gradia understands context:** Show the customer, vehicle, requested service and relevant reviewed business knowledge.
4. **Bounded action:** Show the proposed follow-up, quote or appointment and why it requires approval or is allowed.
5. **Human control:** Approve or edit the exact action. One approval does not silently grant future autonomy.
6. **Durable execution:** Show the run state and visible hold or exception behavior.
7. **Measured outcome:** Show how activity connects to a confirmed result.

If any step is unstable, use recorded screenshots from the exact release candidate and say it is a recorded local acceptance flow. Never describe fixture data as a live customer deployment.

## Architecture explanation

Use this after the business story is understood:

> A model does not directly control the business. Gradia separates reasoning from authority and execution. Events enter a durable workflow layer. The Agent works from tenant-scoped CRM facts, current policies and reviewed memory. It can only call typed, bounded tools. The policy layer determines whether an operation is off, read-only, a private suggestion, approval-required or autonomous. Execution rechecks current authority and business constraints, claims a stable identity to prevent duplicates and records the result for audit and billing.

The important layers are:

1. Intake and normalized events.
2. Durable workflows that can wait, resume and escalate.
3. Tenant-scoped context and reviewed memory.
4. Policy, roles and human approvals.
5. Typed CRM, messaging, quote and booking tools.
6. Outcome, usage and audit evidence.

## Market and go-to-market answer

> We are not starting with a company-wide AI transformation for national enterprises. We are beginning with 5–25-location automotive service groups where an owner or COO can make a decision and one workflow has measurable value. Gradia Consulting helps us discover and implement that workflow. We deploy across 3–10 locations, establish a baseline and measure recovered leads, bookings, response time and administrative hours. When the workflow proves value, we expand across workflows and locations. Those case studies create the path into franchise groups and national networks.

## Business-model answer

> The model combines a recurring platform subscription, billable operator seats and usage or confirmed-outcome charges where the value and evidence are clear. Drafts, failures, uncertain results and duplicate work are not billed. We want strong software margins while keeping price connected to operational value, and we will validate final packaging through the first deployments before broadly publishing every tier.

Do not promise exact future margins without verified provider costs and real usage. Explain that pricing includes margin floors, included usage, caps and auditable settlement designed to prevent uncontrolled cost exposure.

## Likely questions and concise answers

### “How much traction do you have?”

> We are pre-scale on software revenue. The consulting business has created conversations, audits and direct workflow discovery with operators, and we are converting that access into controlled product deployments. The next milestone is repeatable measured outcomes in the first named accounts.

### “Why won't existing CRMs add this?”

> A CRM is usually the system of record, but the work crosses calls, messages, calendars, estimates, approvals and employee knowledge. Gradia coordinates execution across those systems instead of requiring an immediate replacement.

### “Why automotive services?”

> The workflows are frequent, economically measurable and fragmented. Speed-to-lead, estimate follow-up, booking and retention directly affect revenue. The market also contains many distributed operators with similar workflows.

### “What is technically differentiated?”

> Governed execution with business context: durable workflows, typed tools, human approval modes, reviewed memory, tenant isolation and outcome evidence. Gradia is designed to complete work safely, not merely generate text.

### “What has actually been built?”

> We have built the core operating spine and are completing launch acceptance. That includes lead intake, CRM context, agent definitions and revisions, approvals, durable execution foundations, reviewed memory, onboarding measurement, security boundaries and commercial-billing foundations. Provider activation and complete production acceptance remain controlled launch gates.

### “What is still missing?”

> We are finishing signed-in end-to-end acceptance, production provider configuration, Stripe test-mode acceptance, monitoring and support readiness, and final release controls. Longer-term enterprise requirements such as SSO, SCIM, formal compliance evidence and broader integrations follow the first measured deployments.

### “How do you acquire customers?”

> Initially through founder-led sales, the consulting pipeline, industry relationships and targeted outreach to regional operators. Consulting lowers discovery risk and creates the implementation path; software turns repeatable solutions into a scalable product.

### “How big can this become?”

> The entry workflow is lead and customer operations, but the platform can coordinate quoting, scheduling, communication, CRM execution and operational exceptions across distributed automotive service networks. Expansion moves from one workflow to many and from one location group to an enterprise network.
